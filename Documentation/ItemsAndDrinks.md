# Dice, Drinks & Items

## System overview

The GDD proposes three main ways to change the default dice duel: **special dice** alter probability or placement, **drinks** provide temporary abilities and intoxication effects, and **items** manipulate the board or rules. Many of the entries below are **planned concepts**, not verified implemented features.

## Dice types

Standard dice - Ordinary six-sided dice
Fangs - Substitute for a die to force a tie in a pair
One Side dice - Weighted toward a specific face
Jumping dice - Can rotate to a particular face when the table is struck
Special Sides dice - Nonstandard face values, e.g. 2/2/3/4/5/5
Big dice - Occupy two table positions; used by ogres

The GDD describes choosing **six dice** for the active loadout from the collection between brawls.

## Drinks

Drinks grant temporary abilities. The waiter can offer up to three types depending on progression; the player can buy drinks for themselves or for opponents. Drinking affects intoxication, and the draft proposes that a drink's effect may only be discovered after consumption.

### Proposed drink effects

Plus 1 / Plus 2 - Increase the result of one placed die
Reroll 1 / 2 / All - Reroll selected dice before placement
Ogre Strength - Knock out two enemy fangs on a won fight
Darkvision - See in darkness
Denture Drink - Grant an additional health point

### Intoxication thresholds (draft)

Lvl 1 - No visual impact
Lvl 2 - Mild camera sway and burping
Lvl 3 - Stronger sway and blinking
Lvl 4 - Vomiting or falling asleep

The draft proposes that severe intoxication can remove drink bonuses. The exact balancing and duration of effects remain open.

## Items examples

Cleaver - Remove two far-left or far-right dice pairs from scoring
Metal fangs - Gain an additional health point
Cigarettes - Extinguish a table candle 

The GDD distinguishes reusable items with durability, consumables and persistent items with possible cooldowns.

## Cheating and counterplay

The draft includes swapping two dice when the candle is out and the player has darkvision, or when the opponent is distracted or impaired. Other cheating opportunities involve jumping dice or incapacitated opponents. Some enemies, notably trolls, resist darkness-based tricks.

The central design goal is to make a special tool valuable **because of its interaction with a specific rule or opponent**, not simply because it provides a larger number.
