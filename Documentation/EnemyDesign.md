# Enemy & Encounter Design

## Purpose

Opponents are not simply different dice values: the GDD gives them distinct behaviors, vulnerabilities and ways of breaking the standard rules. The intended encounter design forces the player to recognize and adapt to each opponent's particular advantage.

## Base archetypes

**Orcs** Strong, rude, not especially clever; Basic placement behavior; some drink heavily or possess special dice they use poorly
**Ogres** Large, slow and heavy-handed; Use three big dice spanning six slots; cannot become drunk
**Goblins** Clever tricksters; Cheat with modified dice, swap results or exploit distraction; easily intoxicated
**Trolls** More calculating; Plan placements and see in darkness, limiting dark-based cheating

The GDD proposes different fang/health totals across these races and encounter variants.

## Named encounter concepts

### Tutorial orc

Introduces the rules through scripted early losses, removes the player's first fang and informs that other oponents will cheat.

### Odd & Eve

A pair of goblin twins alternating between brawls. One uses odd-valued dice, the other even-valued dice. Winning is intended to reward the player with one die from each twin.

### Old orc lady

A nearby tavern patron who can send the player drinks and potentially become an unusual opponent. Sparing her may unlock help distracting future opponents.

### Goblin banker

Offers optional gold wagers, such as betting on specific rolls or winning using only even values, rather than fighting for fangs.

## Boss concept

**Chef Orc** Uses a cleaver to remove two outermost pairs from scoring; later variant splits dice into two separate three-pair brawls; After defeat player can acquire the cleaver

## Encounter-design pattern

1. **Introduce** to the base dice rules.
2. **Make the exception** through the opponent's identity, animation or behavior.
3. **Force an adaptation** in placement, loadout or resource usage.
4. **Offer tool** that the player may use in later encounters.
